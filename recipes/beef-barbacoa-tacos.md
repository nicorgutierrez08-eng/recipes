Toast the guajillo chiles in a dry pan for 30 seconds per side until fragrant, then soak them in hot water for 10 minutes to soften.

Blend the soaked guajillos, chipotles, garlic, vinegar, cumin, oregano, and beef broth into a smooth, deep-red adobo. Season the sauce well with salt.

Season the beef chunks generously, then sear them in batches in a hot pot until deeply browned on all sides. Don't crowd the pan — browning is where the flavor lives.

Return all the beef to the pot, pour the adobo over it, and tuck in the bay leaves. Pressure cook for 60 minutes, or braise covered in a 300°F (150°C) oven for about 4 hours, until the meat shreds with a fork.

Shred the beef directly in its sauce and let it soak up the braising liquid. Discard the bay leaves. Taste and brighten with a little more vinegar if needed.

Warm the tortillas, then pile with barbacoa and top with diced onion, cilantro, and a squeeze of lime. Leftover beef freezes well and only gets better.
